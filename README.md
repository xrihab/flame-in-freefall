## 🚀 The Problem
For decades, NASA has conducted groundbreaking combustion experiments aboard the International Space Station (ISS), Space Shuttle, and Mir. However, critical safety findings are often locked inside complex academic papers and sprawling technical archives. As humanity prepares for long-duration missions to the Moon and Mars, mission planners, engineers, and educators need fast, intuitive access to these life-saving insights.

## 💡 Our Solution
**Flame in Freefall** transforms complex microgravity fire science into a clean, interactive web experience where users can visually compare flame behavior, explore historic NASA experiments, and access verified mission safety resources in one place.

### ✨ Key Features
* **Earth vs. Microgravity Flame Comparison:** Visualizes how the absence of buoyancy-driven convection changes flame shape, temperature, soot production, and spread rates.
* **NASA Experiment Explorer:** Breaks down key findings from landmark microgravity missions, including:
  * **SoFIE** (Solid Fuel Ignition and Extinction)
  * **FLEX** (Flame Extinguishment Experiment)
  * **BASS** (Burning and Suppression of Solids)
  * **FM2**, **SAME**, and **ACME**
  * Historical safety lessons from **Apollo** and **Mir**
* **Spacecraft & Lunar Habitat Safety Rules:** Translates raw experimental data into clear, actionable design guidelines for low-gravity environments.
* **Verified NASA Resource Hub:** Curates direct links to official NASA datasets, technical reports, and research documentation for deeper exploration.

## 🛠️ Built With

* **Deployment:** GitHub Pages & Vercel
* **Data Sources:** NASA Physical Sciences Informatics (PSI), NASA Technical Reports Server (NTRS), and official 2026 NASA Space Apps Challenge datasets. All sources used are hyperlinked at the end of the website page
